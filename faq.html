Program eligibility

Eligible programs offer an active clinical psychology PhD track and appear in the APA, PCSAS, CPA, or CUDCP directories. CUDCP membership and each accreditation status have separate labels. Combined PhD tracks include faculty listed under the clinical track. CUDCP affiliates outside the United States and Canada can be included using the international toggle. The directory retains historical program entries for lookup.

Core tenure-track and research-track faculty are eligible. Postdocs, graduate students, adjuncts, and emeriti are excluded. A program needs a complete reviewed roster and resolved author records before it can be ranked.

Publication credit

In fractional mode, each author receives 1/n of a paper with n authors. In weighted mode, first and last receive 2/(n+2) each and middle authors receive 1/(n+2). A solo author gets the whole point and two authors split it evenly.

If publisher metadata explicitly marks co-first or co-senior authors, all marked positions receive the outer-author weight, with weights normalized to sum to one. For papers with more than 50 authors, weighted credit is divided equally among the first and last positions and their documented co-first or co-senior positions. Other authors receive zero in weighted mode.

Credit goes to the eligible program at the earliest documented official publication date. Publication affiliations corroborate dated appointment records and can resolve uncertain appointment boundaries. Papers from before an eligible appointment earn no program credit. Work that appears right after a move may reflect research done at the previous lab. The current-faculty toggle keeps publication-time attribution and restricts each program’s count to faculty still on its roster.

Venue lists

The default view targets journal articles and reviews, including meta-analyses. Source publication types serve as a peer-review proxy where item-level evidence is unavailable. PsyArXiv preprints appear in a separate view and contribute zero to journal rankings. Research subfields use work titles and available topic metadata independent of venue.

How recall is measured

A seeded sample of 50 faculty is selected before their five-year CV or lab lists are compared with retrieved publications. The report records the denominator, matched papers, unavailable references, and misses. Fixes are checked against a fresh sample. A sample estimates recall and leaves uncertainty about individual faculty coverage.

Data sources

OpenAlex provides author identities and primary works. PubMed, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, and public CV and lab lists supply cross-checks. The datasets store source links, publication dates, and attribution evidence. The review queue contains unresolved matches and coverage gaps.
